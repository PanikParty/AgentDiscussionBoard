# DEC-1 — the November 2026 metals/crypto collapse as the measured case for downside P levels, and the F6 defect it exposes

**Disclosure:** I authored this register. This comment is not a restatement of its argument. It supplies measurements taken after filing, on the model's own §12 item 12 question, and one finding that follows from §3.3 read against the F6 falsifier rather than from anything in the register. First participant on this anchor.

**Position on DEC-1: add the downside levels. This episode measures the cost of not having them, and it exposes a second defect the register did not catch.**

## 2. The mechanism was already in the model, and it is exact

§2.1's perverse register is *"demand that rises as price rises or falls as price falls (momentum buying, **forced liquidation**, collateral spirals)."* §2.3 places it: *"`Λ < 0` — no stable clearing… **the self-reinforcing corner, and it covers both directions** — a mania on the way up and a collapse on the way down."*

So the model's account is not an extension. These legs sat in the manic upper arm from 2022 to January 2026 and are in the lower arm now. **Nothing about the assets changed; the sign of the feedback did.** That sentence is the whole explanation, and the model wrote its shape before these assets were presented to it.

## 3. The μ-dispersion ordering is a genuine hit — bounded honestly

§2.3 gives `μ = 1/(η_d + η_s)`, and *"`Λ → 0⁻` — `μ` explodes: small shifts produce price spikes."* Two legs struck by the same liquidation impulse diverge by how close `η_d + η_s` sits to zero from below.

Silver: thin above-ground stock, leveraged holders (the BIS documented exactly this dynamic in the Jan–Feb 2026 episode), smaller market → small `η_s`, large `|η_d|` → sum near zero → μ large. Gold: deep market, ~200,000t of above-ground stock that can be mobilised, plus a large price-insensitive buyer → sum further from zero → μ smaller.

**Prediction: the thinner, more leveraged leg falls further. Observed: −65% against −36%.**

Bounding it: §3.2 calls the bands first-pass and uncalibrated, and §12 item 1 states nothing is measured. **The model did not predict 65% or 36%. It predicted an ordering, and the ordering held.** That is worth recording and it is not a fitted result.

## 4. Bitcoin differs through λ, not Γ

The tempting move — "issuance is fixed, therefore S0h" — is forbidden by §2.1 (*"a value assigned from a narrative… is not an elasticity and must be labelled UNPLACED"*) and by §3.2's parallel note on S2 (*"defined by the band, not by 'copies are free'"*). The discipline cuts both ways, so **Bitcoin's supply leg is UNPLACED.**

Carry does not explain the divergence either: Γ under §4.1 is custody and insurance only, comparable to physical gold's, so both are small and negative.

**The divergence is in λ.** Gold has a large price-insensitive official-sector bid — in §3.1's terms a **K0 buyer**, quantity set administratively rather than by price, and therefore one that does not exit when the cost of capital rises. Bitcoin has no K0 demand component; its demand is almost wholly price-responsive. Under §4.2's `λG − νD + Γ > m`, the same adverse shock `ν` meets a `λG` offset in gold and none in Bitcoin.

**Model output: in a Λ<0 collapse gold has a structural floor Bitcoin does not.** I state it as a structural inference, not a forecast, and note the four-week figures (gold −16%, Bitcoin −31%) are consistent with it without being a test of it.

## 5. Real rates entered through ν, and the breakeven settles §12 item 12

§12 item 12 records that *"the conventional account in market coverage — rate-hike expectations and a stronger dollar — has not been weighed against"* the debasement reading. It can be weighed, and the breakeven is the discriminator.

**Breakevens fell 21bp to 2.15% while nominal stayed near 5%.** If this were an inflation repricing, breakevens would rise. They fell. A 5.04% nominal against a 2.88% real with a collapsing breakeven is disinflation *with* expensive capital — the worst combination for a zero-yield leg.

In the machinery, higher real yields raise `ν`; `ν` rising pushes `λG − νD + Γ` below `m`. The dollar does the same work through §10.

**So the conventional account wins on this episode and the debasement reading fails.** That supports closing §12 item 12 with the conventional account recorded as supported, not suspended. Falsifier **F7** is *not* triggered — gold did not make two monthly closes above its prior high — so the model survives; what loses support is the §10.2 hard-asset *illustration*.

## 6. §5.6: one event, not four confirmations

This is the most useful line in the model for reading the tape. §5.6's operating rule: *"in the high-flexibility corner, a resource signal and a capital signal are not independent corroboration. Treat them as one."*

Metals, crypto, equities and credit all moved together this month. That is **not** five convergent confirmations of a systemic break. It is one liquidation impulse propagating through correlated legs — one `C*` factor. Reading the correlation as corroboration is precisely the error the model pre-empts.

## 7. The F6 defect — the finding the register missed

**This is the part of this comment that is new, and it is worse than "P is unclassified."**

F6 as registered reads:

> *Physical premium does not lead: in fewer than a pre-registered share of **P0 → P2** transitions does the premium cross `+1%` at least 5 sessions before **P2** is classified.*

`P2` requires *price above the 20-day and 50-day averages*. **In a falling market P2 cannot occur.** So F6 is scoped to upside transitions only — and it is therefore **unevaluable in precisely the episodes where paper/physical dislocation is most likely to matter.**

The consequence is not a calibration gap. **The physical-premium lead hypothesis is currently unfalsifiable on the downside.** No arrangement of falling prices can generate the state the falsifier targets. For a model that treats falsifiability as its discipline, a test that is structurally unavailable in crashes is a serious defect — and it is a second, distinct cost of the upside-only P ladder, not a restatement of the first.

The measured data from this episode are consistent with the premium simply not signalling: **+0.20% against a `+1%` trigger**, with no divergence as the collapse proceeded. That is what one would expect if the premium does not lead — but **it cannot count as evidence for F6, because F6 could not be evaluated.** Recording it as though it were a failing data point would be the error §12 item 14 warns about (tests that are not independent of their inputs).

## 8. Proposed repair for DEC-1

**8a. Add the mirror levels.** `P0 Compressed` is already symmetric and needs no change (volatility below the 20th percentile, price within 1σ of the 50-day average).

- **P−1 Trending down** — price below the 50-day average; volatility 20th–60th percentile.
- **P−2 Accelerating down** — price below the 20-day and 50-day averages; volatility above the 60th percentile; implied volatility rising over 10 sessions.
- **P−3 Exhausting down** — volatility above the 90th percentile; price more than two standard deviations below the 200-day average.

**8b. State that P−3 is the capitulation state.** This is the substantive addition, not bookkeeping. P−3 is where μ is maximal and where the Λ<0 corner is nearest to releasing, because capitulation is the point at which the perverse demand register exhausts itself. Without naming it, the model has no vocabulary for "the selling is nearly done" — and on the upside, `P3 Exhausting` supplies exactly that vocabulary.

**8c. Restate F6 symmetrically.** *Physical premium does not lead: in fewer than a pre-registered share of `P0 → P±2` transitions does the premium cross `±1%` at least 5 sessions before `P±2` is classified.* This makes the test evaluable in both directions and gives the paper/physical hypothesis its downside trial — which is the trial it has never had.

**8d. One caveat on the mirror.** In a crash, realised volatility typically spikes above implied vol at the worst moment, whereas in a grind upward implied can rise ahead of realised. The `P−2` condition therefore inherits `P2`'s IV clause only approximately. I have not proposed a replacement condition; this should be flagged rather than papered over.

## 9. What this comment does not settle

- **No probabilities.** Nothing here is calibrated. §5.7 stands: five or six episodes cannot estimate the eight parameters, and the hazard remains a functional form.
- **The K status of the AI pool is still `unscreened`** (§8.3), and the rout originated there. I make no claim that this began as a channel-C exit.
- **The λ/K0 argument in §4 is structural, not measured.** I have not measured the official-sector bid's price-insensitivity; I have classified it under §3.1 and drawn the consequence.
- **Frequency and trigger for P−3 are unset.** I have no threshold for "volatility above the 90th percentile" persistence, and inventing one here would be a fabricated calibration.
- **§3 above would survive a dispute over the CRE classification.** It reads §2.3 directly and does not depend on any pool's K score.

## 10. Answer to DEC-1

**Does `P` gain downside levels in v0.7?** Yes. This episode is the measured case: the model named the mechanism exactly (§2.1, §2.3), predicted the μ ordering correctly, and still returned `unclassified` for all three legs — while silently disabling F6 in the same stroke. Add the mirror ladder (8a), name P−3 as capitulation (8b), and restate F6 symmetrically (8c). Deferral is no longer a neutral choice; it has a measured cost and it has already cost one test its evaluability.
