## Agreed on the Status column — but one of the "already decided" tests is decided on inadmissible inputs

The four-way split is right and the Status column is the correct instrument. One correction, and it moves a test from DECIDED to something the column needs a fifth category for.

**The D2 sign test isn't decided; it's decided on inputs D1 forbids.** Your comparison is `ΔR = +0.5` from RCED's single coordinate against `ΔR ≈ −0.30` from §6.1's per-layer reading. Both of those numbers are narrative placements:

- RCED's is the one §2.2 retracts — "placed by narrative, which is why the AI row flipped sign under two defensible readings."
- §6.1's is the one Gurney's A13 finding rules inadmissible under D1 — inferred from the existence of take-or-pay contracts rather than from any price–quantity response, and §10.2 concedes "none of the four have been estimated."

So the sign flips *between two inadmissible readings*. That is not a passing test; it is a demonstration that the metric is unstable under exactly the ambiguity D1 was adopted to remove. If the Status column records it as DECIDED, §9 will read as though D2 has been validated in evidence when it has been resolved only in rhetoric — the failure mode the section's own opening sentence exists to prevent ("The framework must be falsifiable or it is decoration").

Category it as **DECIDED-VACUOUS**, meaning: the test resolves, but against inputs the model's own Decision 1 disqualifies. It becomes genuinely DECIDED once one real per-layer `ε` exists — which is §11.1's first next step. That is a much more useful status than either DECIDED or PENDING, because it tells the reader exactly which measurement would promote it.

**On your "indefinitely" objection — it is fixable rather than fatal.** "K2 persists indefinitely" can be made decidable by binding the horizon to something the document already believes: the lead time of the binding layer. §2.5 puts grid interconnect at 3–5 years and says no price accelerates it. If a K2 state outlives the *longest* lead time of its binding layer, it has persisted indefinitely in the only sense §4.0 needs — the physical clock ran out and nothing broke. That converts an unfalsifiable claim into a dated one with a threshold that is already in the text.

**One dependency you should record.** You rightly class "negative risk-adjusted returns across two full cycles" as UNDERSPECIFIED for want of a benchmark and cycle boundary. It also lacks a *position rule*: risk-adjusted returns are undefined until the model says what it holds in each state. That rule is Gurney's A8 (`x = λG/c > 1`). So A15 → A8, on top of the A9 → A8 and A10 → A8 edges already on the record. The map test cannot be made executable before A8 settles, and A8 cannot settle before A9 and A10.
